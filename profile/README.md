# SETO Engineering

**Independent engineering and open-source development.**

Building practical, reusable tools across software, systems, and hardware.

SETO is an independent engineering initiative focused on developing accessible software, exploring technical systems, and improving the workflows used to build and maintain them.

Our work combines software engineering, automation, developer tooling, and applied engineering. We aim to create solutions that are understandable, maintainable, and useful beyond their original projects.

## What We Do

- **Developer Tools** — Utilities for development workflows, repository analysis, visualization, and automation.
- **Software & Libraries** — Reusable components, packages, and tools designed for integration into other projects.
- **Systems Engineering** — Exploring software integration, embedded systems, and engineering infrastructure.
- **Research & Development** — Investigating technical problems, evaluating approaches, and documenting reproducible solutions.

## Engineering Principles

**Practicality**  
Build solutions that address real problems and remain useful outside their original development environment.

**Transparency**  
Document design decisions, limitations, dependencies, and verification results.

**Reproducibility**  
Prioritize maintainable code, consistent development environments, and testable implementations.

**Accessibility**  
Support free and open-source software, shared knowledge, and opportunities for community participation.

**Continuous Improvement**  
Treat software as an evolving engineering system, incorporating feedback, testing, and ongoing refinement.

## Collaboration

SETO welcomes constructive feedback, technical discussion, and open-source contributions.

Contributions and issues can be submitted through the relevant public repositories. For engineering inquiries or potential collaboration, contact us directly.

## Support Open Source

We believe useful engineering software should remain accessible without requiring payment.

Community participation, feedback, documentation, and voluntary financial support can all help sustain the development and maintenance of open-source projects.
